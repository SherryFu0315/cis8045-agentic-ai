About the course

Most organizations can get an AI agent to work once. Far fewer can get one to work on the thousandth request, inside a budget, with a named person accountable when it goes wrong. This course is about what it takes to get from the first to the second.

You will build an agent in the first week. The remaining weeks turn it into something a company could run. You will give it tools and limits, ground it in the right documents, measure whether it does the job, attack it before anyone else does, and work out what it costs per task and who owns it. Each team finishes with a working service, an evaluation report, a security review and a two-page decision memo written for an executive who has to say yes or no.

The course is for M.S. students who have taken at least one earlier PATH course. Some of you write Python and some of you do not. There is a code path and a no-code path, and they lead to the same deliverables. Every member of a team reads traces and writes.

Four questions

Every deliverable is graded against the same four questions. They appear in the first video, and they come back on the last day, when a panel decides whether your service should go live.

  1. What tools may the loop call?
  2. What is it allowed to touch?
  3. How do we know it did the right thing, reliably, and not once in a demo?
  4. What does it cost, in money and in people's time, to find out?

How a week works

Before class
Two or three short videos (two to eight minutes each), one reading, and a five-question quiz on the videos. The videos page has the scripts and captions.
In class
We meet one evening a week, 5:30 to 9:45. The evening opens with a debrief on the videos and the reading, then one team presents its trace of the week. The lab follows, with a code path and a no-code path. After the break, teams work on that week's deliverable in the studio while I sit with two teams at a time.
After class
Each team submits a one-page deliverable before the next session. The project page lists them.
